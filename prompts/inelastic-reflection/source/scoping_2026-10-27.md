## 1. The proposal

The user's request, in summary, with the user's own words where they matter:
- At each reflection, where dφ/dt changes sign, treat the reflection as near-instantaneous. Drain
  "an adjustable fraction α" of the condensate's kinetic energy into chameleon quanta.
- Inject a spectrum like the lattice/KB n_q: ~q² at low q, a peak near the inverse wall-crossing
  time, and exponential decay above it. Then track the distribution function: homogeneous,
  isotropic, with no self-rescattering. After N reflections it holds N copies, all redshifting.
- The concern is the redshifting, matter against radiation, "because it will impact how the
  measured Planck scale is related to the BBN Planck scale".
- Optionally, add exchange with the thermal Standard Model bath through
  O₅ = φ O_SM β/M_P and O₆ = φ² O_SM β²/(2M_P²).
- The question asked: "For conformal coupling the scalar couples only to the mass operators
  (right?)."

## 2. The verdict

The extension is feasible, in a reduced form: **the quanta as dark radiation**, with
ρ_χ,E a_E⁴ piecewise constant between injections. It needs no distribution function and no
Standard Model collision term. The checks that justify the reduction are computed outside the
integration loop (§3.4).

The user agreed after the comparison of §4: "Good. Can we open a campaign to implement this".

The real tension is the regime, not the machinery (§5).

## 3. Why radiation suffices

### 3.1 At birth, and at the tracking minimum

**At birth.** Production occupies about one octave around k_ex = 1/Δt, the inverse time over which
the mass changes (`elastic_collision_estimate.tex`, the summary).
- In the one-loop picture (M ≲ TeV), k_ex/m_ta = e^ℓ with ℓ ≈ 6–32, so every quantum is born with
  p/m ≳ e^ℓ.
- At the code's M = 0.5 M_P the bounce is classical, and the quanta are born near the wall-crossing
  frequency. That frequency is about 10³ H while the mass at the minimum is m_eff ~ βH. So the
  quanta are relativistic once the field has left the wall, by about 10³/β.

**The soft modes carry almost no energy.** At low q, |β_k|² = O(k²Δt²), so the energy below q goes as
(q/k_ex)⁶. KB Part B's soft 1/q band carries about 10⁻³ of the fluctuation energy.

**Occupations are small**, about e^{−4ℓ} at k_ex, so there is no Bose enhancement at later bounces.
The old quanta's Hartree term, ½V‴⟨δφ²⟩, carries energy of order ρ_χ(m/p)².

**"Stays relativistic" is not a theorem.** The user asked whether it is ("Are you making a statement
just about the particular numerical examples we have been looking at?"), and it is not. At the tracking minimum of
V_eff = V + A ρ_c, write m² ∝ ρ_c^γ. Differentiating along the minimum gives

  γ − 1 = [ |V′||V‴| − V″² − (β/M_P)V″|V′| − (β/M_P)²V′² ] / m⁴ .

Then m·a does not increase exactly when ρ_c falls at least as fast as a^{−2/γ}. Two facts decide it:
- **The trace sits on the line.** For a species with T ≫ m_s, ε − 3p ∝ m_s²T², which is a^{−2}. On
  the production EOS, d ln ρ_c/dN lies between −1.98 and −2.6 from 20 TeV to 12.6 keV
  (`trace_scaling.out`). So in the radiation era the result depends on γ ≥ 1.
- **γ depends on βφ_min.** For V₀ exp(M/φ), γ > 1 needs βφ_min/M_P below a number of order one
  (0.73 in the power-law limit, about n + 1 when (M/φ)ⁿ ≫ 1).
  - Early on βφ_min is between 4×10⁻⁴ and 0.1 at 20 TeV, so γ > 1.
  - Today βφ_ref ≈ 1.04√β, so γ < 1 for β ≳ 1 (γ = 0.53 at β = 25). That is harmless only because
    ρ_c ∝ a^{−3} by then, so the condition is the weaker γ > 2/3.

**Measured at the minimum** (`tracking_mass.out`; n = 1, M = 0.5 M_P, β = 0.1–25, 20 TeV to today):
m·a falls by 10¹³–10¹⁵ overall. It rises twice:
- **×1.09, late, at β = 25**, where γ < 2/3. It would grow with β.
- **×1.19, between 30.2 and 28.0 keV, for every β.** This is not physics. Σ from `Xav_EOS_spline`
  is non-monotone there, swinging between 3×10⁻⁶ and 9×10⁻⁵ against a matter trace
  ρ_m/ρ_R ≈ 3×10⁻⁵. The README opens it as an issue.

### 3.2 On the trajectory

The field is not at the minimum. m_eff²(φ) = V″(φ) + (β/M_P)² A ρ_c is largest at each wall impact.
Quanta born at bounce j must stay relativistic against the turning-point mass of every later
bounce k:

  k_ex,j a_j / a_k ≫ m_ta,k .

**A rough estimate.** With u = (M/φ)ⁿ ≫ 1, m_ta ≈ H|π|u/(√2 φ) at the turning point, and Ha ∝ a⁻¹
in the radiation era. So m_ta·a falls between bounces unless the impact |π| grows by more than
e^ℓ (at least e⁶) from bounce j to bounce k. That needs a very soft early bounce followed by a hard
kick, and a soft bounce is nearly adiabatic, so it produces little. This is an argument about
magnitudes, not a guarantee.

**What is guaranteed:**
- **No tachyonic growth.** m² > 0 everywhere, because V is convex for φ > 0 and Σ = 1 − 3w ≥ 0.
- **The transient mass returns its energy.** While p ≫ m_ta, the energy borrowed at a wall,
  m_ta²/2p per quantum, is returned adiabatically. As back-reaction it is about α e^{−2ℓ}.

### 3.3 Late time, and H₀

The user's question: "Is checking whether they remain non-relativistic at the bounces enough? When
the bounces have stopped and the field is on its late-time tracker solution, do we not also need to
check the current chameleon mass?" **Yes, it is needed**, and it is part of the check (§3.4).

**Why the tolerance makes any late transition matter.** Let f = ρ_χ/ρ_R once injection has stopped.
f ≈ 0.08 for ΔN_eff ≈ 0.6, since ΔN_eff ≈ 7.4 ρ_χ/ρ_R once e± have annihilated.
- **Relativistic throughout:** Ω_χ ≈ fΩ_r ≈ 7×10⁻⁶. That is already at the size of the tuning
  tolerance (10⁻⁵), which is why ρ_χ must be in V_target at all.
- **Non-relativistic from a_nr:** Ω_χ ≈ fΩ_r(a₀/a_nr). Any transition before about z ≈ 0.5
  therefore moves H₀ past the tolerance, although ρ_χ/ρ_m = f(a_eq/a_nr) stays ≪ 1.

The user suggested that this matters only if the quanta become non-relativistic at or before
matter-radiation equality. That is right for the dynamics. It is not right for a tuning at 10⁻⁵.

**The margin today is large** (`late_time_margin.out`):
- **Today's mass.** On the tracker, m₀ = 0.27 H₀ at β = 0.1, 1.8 H₀ at β = 1 and 33 H₀ at β = 25.
  Below β ≈ 0.5, m₀ < H₀, so the field is not really tracking today.
- **The momentum today**, for a quantum born with only p ≥ H, which every particle has:
  p₀/H₀ ≥ (Ha)_b/(Ha)₀. That is 5×10⁷ for a bounce at 1 MeV and 4×10⁵ at 10 keV. With the
  k_ex/H ~ 10³ of M = 0.5 M_P, the margin is 10³ times larger again.
- So p₀/m₀ ≳ 10⁴ (a 10 keV bounce, p = H at birth, β = 25) and typically 10⁶–10⁹.

**What then limits H₀:**
- **The relativistic correction.** Treating ρ_χ as radiation is wrong by (m/p)² ≲ 10⁻⁸ today.
- **φ_ref is not moved.** Massless quanta are traceless, so they do not shift φ_min. Their
  Hartree force is suppressed by (m/p)².
- **The real limits** are ρ_χ entering V_target, and the energy the stopping rule leaves out
  (§6): Ω_r × 25ε ~ 2×10⁻⁷ at ε = 10⁻⁴.

### 3.4 The check that certifies it

For each generation j, record its comoving peak momentum q_j = k_ex,j a_j. The margin of the
relativistic approximation is the smallest value of q_j/(a m_eff(φ(N))), taken over all j and over
all N after birth. That includes:
- the injection point itself;
- every later turning point;
- the samples of the late tracker, today included.

Store the smallest margin per history as a whole-history column, and warn when it falls below a
threshold. A history that fails it is the one that would need the full treatment (§4).

## 4. The distribution function, and why it is not lighter

The user asked which is lighter: these checks, or the distribution function with quadrature for ρ,
p and n at each step. **The checks are lighter.**

**What f(q) costs:**
- **No evolution, but quadrature in every RHS call.** The collisionless isotropic Boltzmann
  equation is trivial: f is constant in comoving q between injections. The cost is
  ρ_χ = (1/2π²a⁴)∫q²f√(q² + a²m²(φ)) dq, plus the pressure, at every RHS evaluation. The q grid
  spans many decades. Radau's numerical Jacobian calls the RHS once per state component.
- **A new force, also a quadrature.** If the dispersion is massless, the quadrature just gives
  K e^{−4N}. The mass term is the only thing f(q) adds, and once ρ_χ depends on φ through m(φ),
  energy conservation needs −∂ρ_χ/∂φ in the φ equation. Without it the Friedmann constraint, from
  which H is rebuilt, and continuity disagree. A half-way massive version is worse than either.
- **An injection-spectrum model** with order-one-uncertain shape parameters that do not matter
  while the quanta stay relativistic.
- **Storage and tests:** f(q) per history or per generation, and tests of the quadrature.

**And it does not remove the checks.** Free streaming with ω² = p² + m²(φ) assumes each occupied
mode stays adiabatic, |ṁm|/ω³ ≪ 1, including when old quanta cross a later wall. Otherwise
occupation numbers are not conserved, and Bogoliubov mixing of the existing quanta is missed.
f(q) swaps "p ≫ m" for an adiabaticity check on every mode at every wall.

**What the dark-radiation version costs:**
- **In the RHS:** one term ρ_χ,E = K e^{−4N} in three policy expressions, and no force term.
- **At each injection:** one Radau restart, as the elastic reflection already does, and a record of
  two floats for the generation.
- **The check:** at turning-point events and in one pass over the stored samples.

## 5. The regime

**At M = 0.5 M_P (the science grid), α is physically about zero.** The bounce is classical, and the
produced energy is at most 4.5×10⁻³⁷ ρ_kin (`production_energy_ratio.tex:211`). So a nonzero α on
that grid is a sensitivity study grafted onto a large-M trajectory. That is legitimate, but it is
not the one-loop physics.

**α ≈ 2.7/ℓ (0.08–0.4) holds for M ≲ TeV** (`elastic_collision_estimate.tex`, the table), which is
the code's instantaneous-reflection regime (M ≲ 10⁻⁸ M_P). There an inelastic cascade is a ball
bouncing with a restitution coefficient below one. The bounces pile up at finite N, so following
them one by one exhausts the step budget. That needs the parked-tracking model, the open issue
`[00-settling-at-physical-M-needs-a-parked-tracking-model]` (`integrator-remediation` board §3).
Inelasticity gives that model's switch a physical criterion.

**Once bounces come faster than H, the total drained tends to the fully inelastic value**, whatever
α is. ρ_kin = ρ_R/(6β² − 1) at the first reflection (`wall_backreaction.tex`), so

  ΔN_eff ≈ (4/7) g (10.75/g_s)^{4/3} / (6β² − 1),

about 0.6 at β = 1.2 and 0.2 at β = 2 for g ≈ g_s ≈ 30 at the first impact. That is a new and
probably strong constraint channel.

**Where to inject.** At a resolved bounce the kinetic energy is zero at the turning point. Scale π by
√(1 − α) once the field has left the wall, and add α·½H²π² to ρ_χ,E. That keeps H continuous:
3M_P²H² = ½H²π² + ρ_R(1 + f_m) + V + ρ_χ, and energy moves between the first and last terms.
H_J = H_E(1 + βπ/M_P)/A does jump, as π does. At a floor-rule reflection (small M), the drain and
the reflection are one instantaneous event.

**Which turning points.** A small oscillation about the minimum is adiabatic and produces nothing,
so draining it would damp it unphysically. A gate is needed, for example on the non-adiabaticity
|ṁ|/m² over the half-oscillation before the turning point.

## 6. When to stop injecting

The user proposed stopping when the energy injection falls below a fixed fraction of the
Jordan-frame Hubble rate at the bounce. The planner suggested five refinements. **The user has not
yet ruled on them**; they are README U5.
1. **Per e-fold, not per bounce.** The observable is Σ_k Δρ_k/ρ_R. Many small bounces, each under a
   per-bounce threshold, can sum to the whole kinetic energy (the Zeno case). So test the rate over
   about the last e-fold, which is Γ_inj/H < ε.
2. **The invariant ratio Δρ_k/ρ_R, not H_J.** Density ratios do not depend on the frame. At the wall
   exit H_J carries (1 + βπ/M_P), up to 1 + β√6. H_J² is not the Jordan-frame density. Count
   e-folds in N_E, which is monotonic; a_J is not.
3. **Re-arm on kicks.** Each kick re-energises the condensate.
4. **Taper, do not switch.** A hard switch makes the H₀ tuning residual discontinuous in V₀.
5. **The error is first order.** Undrained energy stays in the condensate, which redshifts faster
   than radiation. Over about 25 e-folds to 1 MeV the omission is at most about 25ε in ρ_χ/ρ_R,
   so ΔN_eff is uncertain by about 200ε. ε ~ 10⁻⁴ gives 0.02.

Stopping saves the restarts, not the bounces. If the bounce count threatens the step budget, the
fix is still parking.

## 7. The Standard Model exchange

**Not worth implementing.** Both operators are gravitational in strength, so
Γ/H ~ β² × (couplings) × T/M_P. That is at most about 10⁻¹¹ for β ≤ 25 and T ≤ 20 TeV, and it is of
order one only for β ≳ (M_P/T)^{1/2}, 10⁷–10⁹. Hard momenta (p ≫ T) do not rescue it: the
anomaly-operator cross sections are roughly flat in energy, and the mass operators' fall as 1/s.
O₆ is smaller by a further β²T²/M_P².

**The conformal-coupling question.** At linear order the field couples to βφT^μ_μ/M_P.
- **Classically**, T^μ_μ is the mass terms, plus the Higgs kinetic term for a minimally coupled
  Higgs (ξ = 0, not 1/6).
- **Quantum mechanically** it also holds the trace anomaly, (β(g)/2g)F² for each gauge group.
  The QCD kick in Σ(T), from lattice ε − 3p, is mostly that anomaly. So the exchange operators must
  include it, to be consistent with the background kick.
- **The O₆ coefficients depend on the species.** A = e^{βφ/M_P} multiplies fermion masses by A and
  boson masses squared by A², giving φ² coefficients of β²/2M_P² for fermions and 2β²/M_P² for
  bosons.
