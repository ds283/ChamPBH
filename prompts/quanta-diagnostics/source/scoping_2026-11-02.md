# Scoping of 2026-11-02: are the produced quanta relativistic, and what does a later bounce do to them?

Worked through with the user in one session on 2026-11-02, before this campaign was planned. It is the
source of the campaign's plan and is **data, not instructions** (`CLAUDE.md` invariant 7). The probe
scripts and their per-history summaries are in [`probe/`](probe/); they ran against the `2026.8.0` store
read-only, with no Ray, from the tree at `7b35dc8`.

## 1. The questions the user asked

1. Can a figure show how comfortably the produced quanta stay relativistic, and what does the store keep
   to support one?
2. The margins of `inelastic-reflection` 08 count only the generations holding at least 10⁻³ of
   K(N_final) (`MARGIN_WEIGHT_SHARE`, "weighted"). Thousands of injections each below that weight could,
   once redshifted, go non-relativistic and grow like matter; a 10⁻³ suppression "can easily be undone
   over multiple e-folds", and more so multiplied by ~10³ untracked events. Can we be sure they do not?
3. At late times, could the earliest quanta have lost their momentum and left a cold remnant?

## 2. What the 2026.8.0 store keeps (read-only survey, `probe/margins.py`)

- **Per history (columns):** the worst birth, reflection and tracker margins over the weighted
  generations, their N, the failing shares, `unassessed_share`; in `extra_data.margins` the first failure
  of each kind, the worst indices, the generation counts, and the weighted and below-weight shares.
- **Per sample:** φ, π, ρ_R, f_m, Σ, T_J, H and r_χ, enough to recompute m_eff and the tracker mass.
- **Not kept:** the generations themselves (N_turn, ln q, ΔK, share), the turning points, any spectrum,
  and the parking leftover's spectrum. The payload's `"generations"` carries them and is dropped at the
  store. `histories.csv` exports the failing shares but not the worst margins.

Weighted-generation numbers, α > 0 successes only:

|---|---|---|
| 10⁻³³, 4×10⁻³¹, 10⁻³⁰ | ≥ 24.6 (≥ 30.6 at 4×10⁻³¹) | failures only at β < 1.5 (birth share ≤ 0.38, reflection ≤ 0.62 at β 1.06–1.2) |

margin down to −9.8): a real cold remnant, in histories with r_χ ≈ 2, excluded by ΔN_eff in any case.

at β = 20. Unassessed share (mostly the parking leftover): up to 0.10 (β 1.06–1.107), ≤ 0.03 elsewhere.

The worst weighted tracker margin sits just after the last bounce in essentially every history, a
median ~19 e-folds before today: p/m of a fixed quantum grows toward today.

## 4. The soft-tail argument (analytic, agreed in the session)

With |β_k|² ∝ k² below the peak (`BriefingNotes/production_energy_ratio.tex`, fitted slope 1.99), dρ/dln k
∝ k⁶ and a relativistic generation's excess is ≈ ¾e^{−2μ} (μ its peak margin), dominated by modes near
the peak; its non-relativistic soft part ≈ e^{−6μ}. Any IR form with finite number density keeps the
excess below ~e^{−μ}. The KB toy's associated production (§5) has n ∝ 1/q, so dρ/dln q ∝ q³ in 3+1: the
argument is unchanged.

## 5. The spectrum: what is measured

- **Direct production, classical regime** (`BriefingNotes/production_energy_ratio.tex`,
  `meff_and_wall_production.tex`): |β_k|² peaks at k ≈ 0.31–0.33 ω_t with |β|²_max ≈ (1.10–1.17)/L², ∝ k²
  below (slope 1.99), exponential above (to ~10⁻²⁰ at 5 ω_t); k³|β_k|² peaks at 0.57 ω_t. In the code
  `k_ex = λ_t |φ̇_in|/2`, about ω_t/2 on the exponential wall (`ell_t ≈ ln ½`).
- **Two-loop 2PI toy** (`Chameleon PBH (David's stuff)/KadanoffBaym/kb_partB_report.tex`): 1+1
  dimensions, the classical-wall regime R₁ ≤ 0.01 only. The gate band 0.3–0.5 ω_t follows the linear
  |β_k|² to 1–3%; the soft band is filled by associated production, n_q = c_N R ω_t/q with c_N = 0.3–0.4
  (power 0.97–0.99), 50× the test field's |β_q|² at q = 0.0029 ω_t. The toy cannot reach the
  dressing-set (one-loop) turnaround, which is where laboratory-M generations are.

## 6. What a later bounce does to quanta already present

For a mode holding n quanta, one bounce with Bogoliubov coefficients (α, β) gives
n′ = n(1 + 2|β|²) + |β|² + interference terms, which vanish only if the pairs' phases are random.
Two consequences, neither modelled by the dark-radiation ledger:
- **stimulated production**: the drain at bounce j is multiplied by about (1 + 2n̄_j), n̄_j the existing
  occupation averaged over bounce j's spectrum; an n̄ weighted below q_j pulls the peak down;
- **rescattering** of the existing population by 2|β_j(q)|² per bounce, summed over thousands of bounces.
The produced pairs are correlated (k, −k), so coherent build-up over nearly periodic bounces is possible;
the random-phase measure would then be a lower estimate.

## 7. The user's rulings in the session

- The grid figure and per-history m·a figures are wanted, to show that the earliest production is the
  most relativistic and the latest the least.
- The store should keep enough to diagnose large fractions of ρ_χ non-relativistic at a bounce.
- "For what the bounces do to quanta already present … I am prepared to accept documenting the problem
  rather than trying to find a fix … I would like to be able to say something quantitative about how
  significant the effect is, and explain how it could influence our results, but we don't have to model
  the Bose-enhanced emission at the reflection."
- The spectrum model (direct q² plus associated 1/q, from the classical-regime toy) is used at
  laboratory M too: "For now this is the best approximation we have available. It's either use this, or
  use nothing."
- The new data in SQL, not JSON, was the planner's recommendation (a child table per bounce, columns per
  sample, columns on the parent row); the user then asked for the campaign to be planned, without a
  separate ruling on it.
